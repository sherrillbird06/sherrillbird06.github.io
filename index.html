#PRJ-001 Help Desk Simulator — interactive training game PLAYABLE ▶

A browser game where you actually work a support shift. Tickets come in — a locked-out user, a printer that won't respond, a suspicious email, a dead VOIP phone — and you resolve each one by clicking through the real steps in the right order.

The part I care about most: verifying identity before resetting a password. Skip that step in the game and you lose points, because in real life skipping it is how social engineering attacks succeed. Every wrong move explains why it was wrong.

#PRJ-002 Ticket & Audit Log Tracker — GRC compliance tool LIVE DEMO ▶

A working help desk queue with a compliance twist. Log a ticket, move it through Open → In Progress → Resolved → Closed, and every status change is permanently appended to an audit trail that can't be edited or deleted after the fact.

That append-only design is the whole point. An audit log a technician can quietly rewrite isn't an audit log — it's a status field. The export button dumps everything to CSV, which is the format a GRC or compliance reviewer would actually ask for.

#PRJ-003 IT Vocab Trainer — study tool for students SIDE PROJECT ▶

I built this one for myself first. Studying for Network+ meant drilling a lot of terminology — VOIP, VPN, DHCP, peripherals, VLAN, phishing — and flipping paper flashcards wasn't sticking. So I made a version that quizzes me and tracks which terms I keep missing.

Then I realized classmates were struggling with the same list, so I cleaned it up and made it public. Flashcard mode for review, quiz mode for testing, and plain-English definitions written the way I'd actually explain it to someone, not textbook phrasing.

#PRJ-004 VOIP & Connectivity Knowledge Base Article DOCUMENT ▶

Written documentation matters as much as fixing things — if the fix only lives in one technician's head, the next person rediscovers it from scratch. This is a step-by-step KB article for diagnosing VOIP and network connectivity problems.

It walks through physical connections, power-cycling, VLAN assignment, and bandwidth contention, then gives explicit criteria for when to stop and escalate to Tier 2 instead of burning more time.
