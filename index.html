#PRJ-001 Night Shift — a real-time strategy game ▶ PLAY ▶

You're the only technician working the overnight shift in an ops room. Tickets arrive on their own while you work, each with an SLA timer burning down in the background. Let one expire and it drops — costing you reputation you can't get back.

Each ticket plays out as a real conversation with the caller, and every choice you make gets a verdict explaining why it was right or wrong. The clock runs 22:00 to 06:00, the city outside your window shifts from dusk to dawn, and at the end you get a performance review with a letter rank.

Advice like "verify identity first" or "check the simple thing before escalating" only lands once there's a queue stacking up while you're mid-ticket. Press 1–4 to answer with the keyboard.

#PRJ-003 Ticket & Audit Log Tracker — GRC compliance tool LIVE DEMO ▶

A working help desk queue with a compliance twist. Log a ticket, move it through Open → In Progress → Resolved → Closed, and every status change is permanently appended to an audit trail that can't be edited or deleted after the fact.

That append-only design is the whole point. An audit log a technician can quietly rewrite isn't an audit log — it's a status field. The export button dumps everything to CSV, which is the format a GRC or compliance reviewer would actually ask for.

#PRJ-005 Phishing Inspector — spot the red flags ▶ PLAY ▶

Six messages land in a mock inbox. Click any text that looks wrong — a lookalike sender domain, a manufactured deadline, a link that doesn't go where it claims, a file with a double extension — then decide whether to report it or mark it safe.

Two of the six are completely legitimate, and that's the hard part. Over-flagging real mail is its own failure: it floods the reporting channel and trains people to ignore warnings. You're scored on correct calls and flags caught, and penalised for clicking things that were never suspicious.

Every flag you miss is revealed at the end of the round with an explanation of why it mattered.
